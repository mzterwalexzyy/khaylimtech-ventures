# Job Search Workflow: Resume to Offer

A step-by-step playbook synthesizing all resume and job search skills into one complete journey. Follow the phases in order. Each step references the skill that powers it.

---

## Overview

```
PHASE 0 → PHASE 1 → PHASE 2 → PHASE 3 → PHASE 4 → PHASE 5 → PHASE 6
Clarity    Build     Presence  Target    Apply     Interview  Secure

Career  →  Resume  →  Digital  →  Jobs   →  Apply  →  Prep  →  Offer
Clarity    Writing    Brand      Analysis   Docs      & Nail    & Sign
```

**Estimated time to complete all phases:** 2–4 weeks for setup, then ongoing per application

---

## PHASE 0 — Career Clarity (Do This First If Changing Roles/Industries)

> Skip to Phase 1 if you're staying in your current field.

**Skill:** `career-changer-translator`

Career transitions fail when candidates use their old industry's language in their new target industry. Fix that first.

### Step 0.1 — Map Your Transferable Skills

Identify what you actually own vs. what the title implied:

| Old Role Term | Transferable Skill | New Industry Term |
|---|---|---|
| "Managed team" | People leadership | "Led cross-functional team" |
| "Taught curriculum" | Instructional design | "Designed learning programs" |
| "Military command" | Operations leadership | "Directed high-stakes operations" |

**Categories of universal transferable skills:**
- Leadership & team development
- Analytical thinking & data interpretation
- Communication & stakeholder management
- Process design & operational efficiency
- Budget oversight & resource allocation
- Project delivery & risk management

### Step 0.2 — Identify Your Target Roles

Before writing a single bullet, define:
- **Primary target:** Exact job title you're pursuing
- **Industry:** Sector and company stage (startup, enterprise, non-profit)
- **Seniority level:** IC, manager, director, executive
- **Geography:** Location or remote preference

Knowing these three things determines every decision in the workflow.

### Step 0.3 — Address the Gap Narrative

Prepare a 2–3 sentence explanation of your transition using this framework:

```
DISCOVERY → CONNECTION → ACTION → VISION

"After 8 years in [old field], I recognized [discovery about new field].
My [specific transferable experience] maps directly to [target role challenge].
I've since [specific action taken: certification, project, course].
I'm targeting [specific role type] where I can apply both perspectives."
```

## PHASE 1 — Build Your Resume

> Goal: One polished master resume that is ATS-safe, achievement-focused, and truthful.

### Step 1.1 — Choose Your Resume Type

**Skill:** `resume-section-builder`

Select the format that fits your situation:

| Situation | Format | Lead With |
|---|---|---|
| 5+ years in same field | Chronological | Most recent role |
| Career changer | Hybrid/Functional | Skills & achievements |
| Recent graduate | Chronological | Education + projects |
| Executive (15+ years) | Executive | Career highlights |
| Technical role | Chronological | Technical skills section |
| Creative role | Two-version | ATS + designed |

### Step 1.2 — Build Each Section

**Skill:** `resume-section-builder`

**Standard section order:**

```
1. Contact Information
2. Professional Summary (3–4 lines)
3. Skills
4. Professional Experience
5. Education
6. Certifications / Additional
```

**Professional Summary formula:**
```
[Job Title/Identity] + [Years/Seniority] + [Key Skills] + [Value Proposition]

Example:
"Product Manager with 6 years scaling B2B SaaS products from 0 to enterprise.
Expertise in API products, Agile delivery, and cross-functional alignment.
Track record of reducing churn by 20%+ through data-driven roadmap decisions."
```

**Avoid in summary:** "Seeking a challenging opportunity," "passionate team player," "results-oriented professional"

### Step 1.3 — Write Achievement Bullets

**Skill:** `resume-bullet-writer`

Every bullet must show impact, not duty. Use the X-Y-Z formula:

```
"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"
```

**Transformation examples:**

```
❌ WEAK: "Responsible for managing team"
✅ STRONG: "Led cross-functional team of 12 to deliver $2M product launch, 3 weeks ahead of schedule"

❌ WEAK: "Helped with customer service"
✅ STRONG: "Resolved 50+ tickets daily, improving CSAT from 3.2 to 4.8 / 5 over 6 months"

❌ WEAK: "Worked on improving processes"
✅ STRONG: "Redesigned approval workflow, cutting cycle time 40% (10 days → 6 days) across 3 departments"
```

**Strong action verbs by category:**

| Led | Built | Grew | Saved | Improved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Directed | Launched | Scaled | Reduced | Optimized |
| Managed | Architected | Expanded | Eliminated | Streamlined |
| Championed | Developed | Increased | Cut | Accelerated |

### Step 1.4 — Quantify Everything

**Skill:** `resume-quantifier`

Six metric categories to mine from every role:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Money** | Revenue generated, budget managed, cost saved |
| **Time** | Hours saved per week, cycle time reduced, on-time delivery |
| **Percentages** | Growth rate, efficiency gain, error reduction |
| **Volume** | Team size, customers served, projects managed |
| **Quality** | Satisfaction scores, accuracy rates, NPS |
| **Frequency** | Daily/weekly output, calls per day |

**When you don't have exact numbers, estimate conservatively:**
- Use ranges: "8–12 clients per month"
- Use minimums: "100+ SKUs"
- Calculate from known data: "Team of 5 × avg salary = $500K headcount I managed"

### Step 1.5 — Role-Specific Optimization

**Tech roles → `tech-resume-optimizer`**
- Lead with technical skills grouped by category (Languages, Frameworks, Cloud, Tools)
- Bullets follow the formula: `[Action Verb] + [Technical What] + [Scale/Impact]`
- Include GitHub, portfolio, and project links
- Example: "Reduced API latency by 60% by migrating from REST to GraphQL, serving 1M+ daily requests"

**Executive roles → `executive-resume-writer`**
- Lead with Career Highlights section above experience
- Use strategic verbs: orchestrated, architected, championed, transformed
- Every role needs company context: revenue, headcount, reporting line
- Formula: `[Leadership Action] + [Strategic Initiative] + [Business Outcome at Scale]`

**Academic roles → `academic-cv-builder`**
- CV, not resume — 2–20+ pages is appropriate
- Order: Contact → Education → Publications → Research → Grants → Teaching → Service
- Science: author order matters; Humanities: solo monographs take priority

**Creative roles → `creative-portfolio-resume`**
- Maintain TWO versions: ATS-safe (.docx) and designed (.pdf)
- ATS version: single column, standard fonts, no tables or images
- Designed version: for portfolio sites, direct email, and in-person meetings
- Always include portfolio link prominently

### Step 1.6 — Format for ATS

**Skill:** `resume-ats-optimizer` + `resume-formatter`

**Non-negotiable ATS rules:**

```
✅ File: .docx or text-based .pdf
✅ Fonts: Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Times New Roman (10–12pt body)
✅ Layout: Single column
✅ Contact info: In document body, not header/footer
✅ Section headers: "Professional Experience" not "Where I've Been"
✅ Bullets: •  -  * only
✅ Dates: Consistent format (MM/YYYY or just YYYY)

❌ No tables, text boxes, or columns
❌ No images, icons, skill bars
❌ No headers/footers for key content
❌ No unusual fonts or special characters
```

**ATS match score target: 80%+**

```
Match Score = (Keywords Matched / Total Required Keywords) × 100
```

Keyword placement priority:
1. Professional Summary (5–8 keywords)
2. Skills section (exact phrasing from job description)
3. Experience bullets (woven in naturally, 2–4× for critical terms)

### Step 1.7 — Set Up Your Master Resume

**Skill:** `resume-version-manager`

Your master resume is your source of truth — never submitted directly, never edited for a single job.

**Folder structure:**
```
/Resume
  /Master
    Master_Resume_[YourName].docx    ← Full archive, never submit
  /Tailored
    /[CompanyName]_[Role]_[Date]
      Resume.pdf
      CoverLetter.pdf
  /Applications
    tracker.xlsx                      ← Log every application
```

**Tracking spreadsheet columns:**
```
Company | Role | Resume Version | Applied Date | Status | Follow-Up Date | Notes
```

## PHASE 2 — Build Your Digital Presence

> Goal: Recruiters find you AND your online presence validates your resume.

### Step 2.1 — Optimize LinkedIn

**Skill:** `linkedin-profile-optimizer`

LinkedIn is not a copy-paste of your resume. It works differently:

| Element | Resume | LinkedIn |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 1–2 pages | Unlimited |
| Tone | Formal | Conversational |
| Keywords | Job-specific | Industry-wide |
| Audience | One employer | All recruiters |

**Critical LinkedIn sections:**

**Headline (220 characters):**
```
Formula: [Role] | [Key Expertise] | [Value Proposition]

Example: "Product Manager | API & Developer Tools | Scaling B2B SaaS 0→Enterprise"
```

**About section (first 300 characters are shown before "see more"):**
```
Hook immediately. Lead with who you are, what you do, and what drives you.
Total: 1,500–2,000 characters across 3–5 paragraphs.
End with a CTA: "Open to Product Manager roles — DM me or reach out at email@example.com"
```

**Other quick wins:**
- Professional headshot (21× more views)
- Custom background banner
- All 50 skill slots filled and ordered by relevance
- Open to Work enabled (recruiter-only for discretion)
- 5–10 quality recommendations

### Step 2.2 — Build Portfolio Case Studies

**Skill:** `portfolio-case-study-writer`

Essential for PMs, UX designers, engineers, marketers, and creative roles. Convert your best resume bullets into full narratives.

**Case study structure:**
```
1. OVERVIEW — Context and your role (2–3 sentences)
2. PROBLEM — What needed solving and why it mattered
3. PROCESS — Your methodology and key decisions
4. SOLUTION — What you built/delivered
5. RESULTS — Quantified outcomes
6. LEARNINGS — What you'd do differently
```

**Length:** Target 3–5 minute reads. Include screenshots, before/after comparisons, data charts.

**Role-specific focus:**
- PMs: strategy, metrics, stakeholder decisions
- Designers: research, usability improvements, design iterations
- Engineers: architecture, performance, technical tradeoffs
- Marketers: targeting, ROI, channel performance

### Step 2.3 — Prepare Your References

**Skill:** `reference-list-builder`

Don't wait until the final round. Prepare now.

**Reference priority order:**
1. Former direct managers (most important)
2. Senior colleagues who can speak to your impact
3. Peers who worked closely with you
4. Clients or stakeholders (if relevant)

**Reference entry format:**
```
[Full Name], [Current Title]
[Company]
[Phone] | [Professional Email]
Relationship: [Your manager at X Company, 2020–2023]
```

**Pre-brief every reference:**
- Send them your current resume
- Share the job description you're pursuing
- Highlight 2–3 achievements you'd like them to mention
- Confirm they're comfortable being contacted

## PHASE 3 — Target and Analyze Jobs

> Goal: Apply to fewer jobs, better. Quality over volume.

**The strategic reality:**
- Average job gets 250+ applications
- 75% of resumes rejected by ATS before human review
- Top performers apply to 10–15 targeted roles, not 50+
- Target: 70–90% match score per application

### Step 3.1 — Analyze Every Job Posting Before Applying

**Skill:** `job-description-analyzer`

Before spending any time tailoring, run this analysis:

**Match score calculation:**
```
Required Skills Match × 70% + Preferred Skills Match × 30% = Overall Score

90–100%  → Overqualified (may be seen as flight risk)
75–89%   → Excellent fit — apply immediately
60–74%   → Good fit — strong cover letter needed
50–59%   → Stretch role — apply only if genuinely passionate
< 50%    → Skip (or dream job exception)
```

**Red flags to detect in job postings:**

| Phrase | What It Often Signals |
|---|---|
| "Wear many hats" | Understaffed, unclear scope |
| "Fast-paced environment" | High pressure, potential burnout |
| "Like a family" | Poor work/life boundaries |
| "Competitive salary" | Won't share range up front |
| "Rockstar/Ninja/Guru" | Culture immaturity |
| "Unlimited vacation" | Pressure not to take it |

**Output from analysis:**
- Match score
- Required skills you have vs. missing
- Gaps: critical (dealbreaker), major (addressable), minor (ignore)
- Resume customization priorities
- Cover letter talking points
- Application recommendation: apply / apply with caveats / skip

### Step 3.2 — Tailor Your Resume

**Skill:** `resume-tailor`

Tailoring is about strategic highlighting, not fabrication. You have a library of experience — select the right books for this employer.

**What to adjust per application:**

| Section | How to Tailor |
|---|---|
| Professional Summary | Rewrite to mirror the top 3 role requirements |
| Skills | Reorder — most relevant to this role first |
| Experience order | Lead with most relevant role, not always most recent |
| Bullet points | Swap in bullets that match the job's priorities |
| Keywords | Add exact phrasing from the job description |

**Acceptable vs. unacceptable tailoring:**

```
✅ Acceptable:
- Reordering your experience
- Swapping which bullets you lead with
- Using the job posting's exact terminology
- Emphasizing relevant achievements
- Adding keywords you legitimately have

❌ Not acceptable:
- Adding skills you don't have
- Fabricating metrics or outcomes
- Claiming roles you didn't hold
- Misrepresenting dates or titles
```

**Tailoring checklist per application:**
- [ ] Summary mentions the job title and top 2 skills from JD
- [ ] Most relevant experience appears first
- [ ] Top missing keywords are now naturally included
- [ ] All bullets are still truthful
- [ ] File named: `[LastName]_[Role]_[Company]_[Date].pdf`
- [ ] Logged in tracking spreadsheet

## PHASE 4 — Apply

> Goal: Submit applications that stand out at every touchpoint — resume, cover letter, forms, and outreach.

### Step 4.1 — Write a Targeted Cover Letter

**Skill:** `cover-letter-generator`

Use AFTER the job description analysis. You already have the talking points.

**Cover letter structure:**
```
[Opening: Hook — 2–3 sentences]
[Body 1: Your strongest qualification match — 3–4 sentences]
[Body 2: Secondary value + address any gap — 3–4 sentences]
[Closing: Enthusiasm + call to action — 2–3 sentences]

Total: 250–400 words
```

**5 opening hook types (use only one):**

```
1. SPECIFIC COMPANY KNOWLEDGE
"I saw TechCorp's API marketplace launch last quarter — as a PM who built
a developer tools platform for 2 years, I'd love to bring that experience
to your platform team."

2. MUTUAL CONNECTION
"[Name] on your team mentioned you're looking for a PM to lead the
payments product. Having worked with [Name] at [Company], I know the
team's bar, and I think I can meet it."

3. PROBLEM-SOLVER
"Your JD mentions the challenge of aligning technical and business
stakeholders — I've navigated this in 6 concurrent product launches
at [Company]."

4. IMPRESSIVE ACHIEVEMENT
"In my current role, I reduced churn by 22% in 4 months by rebuilding
the onboarding flow from scratch. I'd love to bring that same focus to
your retention challenge."

5. INDUSTRY INSIGHT
"The shift toward usage-based pricing in SaaS is accelerating — I've
been living that transition for 2 years and have opinions on what works."
```

**Quality checklist:**
- [ ] Opens with a specific hook, not "I am writing to apply"
- [ ] Demonstrates real company research
- [ ] Connects your experience to their specific needs
- [ ] Includes at least one quantified achievement
- [ ] Addresses any significant gap honestly
- [ ] Ends with a clear next step
- [ ] Zero errors (grammar, spelling, name/company accuracy)
- [ ] 250–400 words

### Step 4.2 — Fill Application Forms Precisely

**Skill:** `application-form-filler`

Form fields are not mini cover letters. Answer exactly what's asked, nothing more.

**Question type guide:**

| Question Type | Approach |
|---|---|
| Experience with X | Lead with years + one project + measurable impact |
| Why this company | Specific, not boilerplate. Name a product, initiative, or decision they made. Under 150 words |
| Tell us about yourself | Current role → prior context → why this role. 100–200 words |
| Behavioral/situational | Condensed STAR: 150–250 words with a metric |
| Technical skills | Years + context + honest proficiency rating |
| Opinion/perspective | Take a genuine, grounded stance. Show domain knowledge |

**Non-negotiable form rules:**
- Never repeat the job description back verbatim
- Never use "I'm a fast learner" or other trait claims without proof
- Be honest about gaps — inflation catches up in interviews
- Active voice throughout
- Write like a real person, not a template

### Step 4.3 — Cold Outreach to Hiring Managers

**Skill:** `cold-email-writer`

Cold emails fail because they sound like cover letters. They succeed when they feel like a colleague sharing something interesting.

**Research required before writing:**
- Recipient's name and title
- One specific, non-obvious angle about the company (product decision, market move, recent launch)
- Your one relevant project that matches their domain
- Any genuine technical or strategic overlap

**Email structure:**
```
SUBJECT: [Simple — just your name and role, or a specific observation]

[2–3 sentence hook: specific observation about THEIR company, not generic praise]

[2–3 sentences: your relevant background — current role + 1 specific project]

[1 sentence: the connection — why their problem matches your experience]

[Low-pressure close: "Would love a 20-minute call if timing works — no pressure either way."]

Total: 200–300 words maximum
```

**Hard rules:**
- One specific company angle per email — nothing generic
- Never: "I would be an exceptional addition"
- Never: "I look forward to hearing from you" (dead language)
- Every sentence must be specific to THIS company
- Research thoroughly before writing a single word

## PHASE 5 — Interview

> Goal: Walk in prepared. Walk out confident. Get to the next round.

### Step 5.1 — Analyze the Role for Interview Questions

**Skill:** `interview-prep-generator`

Every job description signals what they'll ask. Extract the competencies:

| What the JD Says | What They'll Ask About |
|---|---|
| "Cross-functional leadership" | Tell me about a time you aligned stakeholders |
| "Data-driven decisions" | Walk me through how you use data to prioritize |
| "Ambiguous environment" | Describe a time you had to act with incomplete information |
| "Customer obsession" | Tell me about a time you went deep on customer research |
| "Fast-paced" | Tell me about managing competing priorities |

### Step 5.2 — Build Your STAR Story Bank

**Skill:** `interview-prep-generator`

Build 8–10 stories before any interview. Each story maps to multiple questions.

**STAR structure:**
```
SITUATION (1–2 sentences): Set the context
TASK (1 sentence): Your specific responsibility
ACTION (2–3 sentences): What YOU did (not "we")
RESULT (1–2 sentences): Quantified outcome + business impact
```

**Story bank — competencies to cover:**

| Competency | Story You Need |
|---|---|
| Leadership | Led a team through a challenge |
| Conflict | Managed a difficult colleague or disagreement |
| Failure | Made a mistake, learned, applied the learning |
| Achievement | Exceeded expectations with measurable impact |
| Problem-solving | Fixed a broken process or complex problem |
| Collaboration | Influenced without authority across teams |
| Initiative | Took on something unprompted, delivered value |
| Prioritization | Managed competing demands, made hard tradeoffs |

**Each story needs three versions:**
- **Full (2 min):** For "tell me about a time..."
- **Short (60 sec):** For follow-up probes
- **One-liner (15 sec):** For "give me an example quickly"

### Step 5.3 — Prepare Your Standard Answers

**"Tell me about yourself" — 2-minute pitch:**
```
STRUCTURE:
1. Current role + biggest recent win (30 sec)
2. Relevant prior experience that led here (45 sec)
3. Why this role at this company specifically (45 sec)
```

**"Why are you leaving?" — Keep it forward-looking:**
```
"I've learned a great deal at [Company], and I'm proud of [specific achievement].
I'm looking for [specific growth area] that isn't available in my current path.
This role at [Target Company] offers exactly that — the chance to [specific opportunity]."
```

**"What's your greatest weakness?" — Real + self-aware + improving:**
```
[Genuine weakness] + [Why you recognized it] + [Specific steps you're taking]

Example: "I tend to over-prepare, which has occasionally slowed me down on lower-stakes
decisions. I've been working on this by setting 80% confidence thresholds for routine
decisions and reserving deep-dive analysis for genuinely high-impact ones."
```

**"Tell me about a failure" — Must be real:**
```
[Actual failure, not a humble brag] + [Specific impact] + [What you learned] + [How you applied it]
```

### Step 5.4 — Research the Company

Before every interview, gather:

- **Recent news:** Product launches, funding, leadership changes, earnings
- **Products:** Understand their core offering and business model
- **Competitors:** Know the landscape they operate in
- **Culture:** Glassdoor patterns (not just scores), LinkedIn tenure data
- **Your interviewer:** Their background, tenure, what they've built

### Step 5.5 — Prepare Questions to Ask

**For Hiring Manager:**
- "What does success look like in this role at 30, 60, and 90 days?"
- "What are the biggest challenges facing the team right now?"
- "How is performance measured and reviewed?"

**For Team Members:**
- "What's the real day-to-day like vs. what the job description says?"
- "What would you want a new hire to know going in?"
- "What do you like most about working here?"

**For Executives:**
- "How does this team contribute to the company's one-year goals?"
- "What keeps you excited about where the company is headed?"

**Never ask:** Salary/benefits (save for recruiter), things you can Google, yes/no questions

### Step 5.6 — Post-Interview Protocol

- Send a thank-you email within 24 hours — specific to what was discussed
- Follow up if you haven't heard within the stated timeline (usually 1 week)
- Update your tracking spreadsheet with status and notes

## PHASE 6 — Negotiate and Secure the Offer

> Goal: Accept the right offer at the right compensation. Never leave money on the table.

### Step 6.1 — Research Market Compensation

**Skill:** `salary-negotiation-prep`

Do this research BEFORE you receive an offer. You need a number ready.

**Sources (use at least 3):**
- Levels.fyi — best for tech roles
- Glassdoor — general market, use as directional
- LinkedIn Salary — based on actual profiles
- Blind — anonymous peer reports
- H1B salary database — publicly filed data

**Build your range:**
```
25th percentile (floor):     $XXX,XXX
50th percentile (target):    $XXX,XXX
75th percentile (strong ask): $XXX,XXX
90th percentile (stretch):   $XXX,XXX
```

**Calculate total compensation, not just base:**
```
Base Salary:           $XXX,XXX
Target Bonus (X%):     $XX,XXX
Equity/RSUs (annual):  $XX,XXX
401k Match (X%):       $X,XXX
Benefits value:        ~$XX,XXX
─────────────────────────────
Total Annual Comp:     $XXX,XXX
```

### Step 6.2 — Negotiate Strategically

**Skill:** `salary-negotiation-prep`

**The non-negotiable rule:** Always negotiate. 84% of employers expect it.

**Timing:** After written offer, before you sign.

```
Day 1:    Receive verbal offer
          → Express enthusiasm, ask for written offer
          → Do NOT accept on the spot

Day 1–3:  Research + prepare counter
          → Verify market rate
          → Calculate total comp
          → Identify your priorities and walk-away point

Day 3–5:  Send counter
          → Email or call
          → Be specific with numbers
          → Anchor on your target, not their offer

Day 5–10: Discussion
          → May take multiple rounds
          → Stay positive and professional

Day 10+:  Resolution
          → Get final terms in writing
          → Review offer letter carefully before signing
```

**Counter-offer email structure:**
```
Express genuine enthusiasm → Reinforce specific value → State specific ask
→ Provide brief justification → Open the conversation
```

**If they won't move on base salary, negotiate:**
- Signing bonus (easiest to approve, one-time cost)
- Additional equity or RSUs
- Earlier performance review (accelerated raise)
- Extra vacation days
- Remote work flexibility
- Professional development budget
- Title upgrade
- Relocation assistance

**Hard rules:**
- ✅ Get everything agreed in writing
- ✅ Be specific (say $X, not "a bit more")
- ✅ Express genuine enthusiasm throughout
- ❌ Never lie about competing offers
- ❌ Never accept verbal-only promises
- ❌ Never give your current salary if you can avoid it (illegal to ask in many states)
- ❌ Never issue ultimatums

### Step 6.3 — Compare Multiple Offers

**Skill:** `offer-comparison-analyzer`

If you have more than one offer, never choose on base salary alone.

**Side-by-side comparison template:**

| Component | Offer A | Offer B | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base Salary | $XXX | $XXX | High |
| Target Bonus | X% | X% | Medium |
| Equity (annual value) | $XXX | $XXX | High |
| Signing Bonus | $XXX | $XXX | Low |
| 401k Match | X% | X% | Medium |
| Remote policy | Hybrid | Full remote | Personal |
| PTO | 3 weeks | Unlimited | Personal |
| Growth potential | High | Medium | High |
| Company stability | Series B | Public | High |
| **Year 1 Total Comp** | **$XXX** | **$XXX** | |

**Beyond the numbers — weighted scoring:**

Score each offer 1–10 on:
- Career growth trajectory
- Team quality and culture fit
- Company financial stability
- Work-life balance
- Role scope and ownership
- Mission alignment

Sum the weighted scores. The highest total compensation is not always the right answer.

### Step 6.4 — Accept and Close

Once you've agreed on terms:
1. Review the written offer letter carefully — verify every agreed number
2. Confirm start date, role title, and reporting structure
3. Request any verbal promises be added to the letter
4. Sign and notify other companies you're withdrawing
5. Give appropriate notice at current employer

## Master Checklist

### Phase 0 — Clarity
- [ ] Identified target role, industry, seniority, and geography
- [ ] Mapped transferable skills to new industry language (if changing fields)
- [ ] Prepared career transition narrative

### Phase 1 — Build
- [ ] Chose resume format for your situation
- [ ] Wrote ATS-compliant sections with standard headers
- [ ] Every bullet uses active verbs and shows quantified impact
- [ ] Zero weak bullets (no "responsible for", "helped with", "assisted")
- [ ] Role-specific optimization complete (tech / exec / academic / creative)
- [ ] ATS compatibility check passed (80%+ match on target roles)
- [ ] Master resume saved — never submitted directly
